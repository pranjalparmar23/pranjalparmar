Automated Detection of Steel Manufacturing Defects Using Machine Learning Techniques

Published at the International Conference on Emerging Technologies for Sustainable Development (ICETS 2025). Proposes a machine learning pipeline — CNN for defect classification, ResUNet for image segmentation, and ResNet50 for defect localization — trained on a dataset of 12,997 steel images.

Pranjal Parmar and Team

Read Paper DOI: 10.35291/icets2025/0031

Abstract

Overview

The combination of changes in raw materials, procedures, and environment increases the difficulty of ensuring product quality in the steel production industry, leading to common flaws such as fractures, inclusions, and inconsistent thickness. Traditional manual inspection methods had less than 70% accuracy, resulting in costly rework and operational inefficiencies. This paper proposes a model-based approach — combining ResNet50, ResUNet, and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) — to accurately detect, predict, and mitigate production flaws in steel, shifting quality control from reactive inspection to proactive, automated monitoring.

This paper is the research companion to the Steel Manufacturing Defects project.

Methodology

Approach

Dataset

12,997 steel images spanning varied lighting conditions, surface types, and textures — 7,095 defective, 5,902 non-defective.

Classification

A CNN model classifies each image as defective or non-defective with 87% accuracy.

Segmentation

ResUNet segments defect regions using Run-Length Encoding (RLE), achieving 76% accuracy.

Localization

ResNet50 pinpoints the precise defect location on the steel surface with 88% accuracy.

Conclusion

Impact

Combining machine learning, artificial intelligence, and image segmentation improves operational and product reliability by shifting from traditional reactive quality control policies to proactive maintenance and optimization — reducing waste generated in steel manufacturing and supporting sustainable development.
